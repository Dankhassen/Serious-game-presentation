Research problem02 / 15
Why history is a difficult subject for games

History education is not only about transmitting facts

Bjørner frames historical learning as a form of historical thinking: understanding context, perspective, evidence and interpretation. This matters because games can show historical spaces, but they do not automatically teach historical reasoning

Content problemHistory often becomes text-heavy and teacher-centered, especially when the past is reduced to names, dates and events
Experience problemThe past cannot be directly experienced. Digital environments can make it visible, spatial and navigable
Evidence problemEngagement is easier to observe than learning. Enjoyment does not prove durable understanding

Thomas Bjørner, A Systematic Review of Examples and the Effectiveness of Game- Based Learning Used To Teach History in K-12 Education

From factual recall to historical thinking
This slide establishes that a historical game should not be judged only by visual accuracy. The deeper question is whether the game supports historical understanding
Bjørner’s systematic review03 / 15
Methodological filter

A large field becomes very small when evidence is required

Bjørner’s review starts with 688 records and narrows them to only 12 studies that fit the criteria: digital game-based learning, history or cultural heritage, K-12 education and implemented learning contexts

This matters for our case. Discovery Tour can be presented as a strong design example, but Bjørner’s filter reminds us not to treat visual quality as proof of learning effectiveness

Systematic review logic
Mention that the review is useful because it is not just descriptive. It asks what methods are used to evaluate learning outcomes and how effective GBL appears to be

Main findings05 / 15
What Bjørner finds

Motivation is the strongest result. Learning effectiveness is less settled

Almost all included studies report positive motivation, engagement or perception when games are used in history teaching. This is the clearest pattern in the review

The evidence for measurable learning outcomes is weaker. Some studies show significant gains, some show no difference compared with regular instruction, and many rely on limited samples or self-reporting

Interpretation

The research field supports the educational promise of historical games, but not a simple claim that games reliably outperform traditional teaching

For a serious game, engagement is necessary but not sufficient. The educational structure still needs to be explicit enough to support learning, assessment and classroom use

Promise without overclaiming
This is a key synthesis point. Present it calmly. Engagement is a valuable educational condition, but it is not equivalent to measured learning
Evaluation challenge06 / 15
What is actually measured

The evaluation methods often measure short-term response rather than durable learning

Common methodsQuestionnaires, knowledge tests, pre/post-tests, interviews, observations, focus groups and think-aloud notes
Methodological limitsSmall samples, different settings, limited randomization, direct knowledge questions and no longitudinal studies
Learning objectivesBjørner notes that specific learning objectives are rarely included clearly in the games themselves
Age and measurementEven Likert-scale questionnaires can be questionable with younger pupils if the scale or questions are not well matched to cognitive ability
Measurement shapes the conclusion
This slide prepares the transition. Discovery Tour can be strong as a design example while still requiring careful evaluation as an educational intervention
Synthesis of Bjørner07 / 15
From review to case selection

A strong serious game should solve a design problem and remain open to an evidence problem

Bjørner’s review suggests a useful standard for judging a historical serious game. It should use the affordances of games, such as space, narrative and interaction, while making educational goals visible enough to be studied

ImmersionHistorical spaces should be experienced as environments, not only as text
StructureLearning content should be organized into clear, accessible units
AssessmentClaims about learning should be separated from claims about attention and interest
The review becomes an analytical lens
This is the bridge. Say that Discovery Tour is interesting because it is not a small prototype. It is a high-budget attempt to design history learning inside an existing historical world
Case transition08 / 15
Discovery Tour

A rare AAA-level educational case in serious gaming

Assassin’s Creed Origins already had a reputation for historical environments, monuments and contextual information. Discovery Tour goes further by turning the game world into a non-combat educational mode

The result is not a conventional school game. It is a commercial open world redirected toward guided historical learning

From research gap to high-budget example
Avoid sounding promotional. The point is analytical: production scale and expert involvement make this case unusual compared with many small educational prototypes
What the game changes09 / 15
Design conversion

Discovery Tour removes the usual game pressure and keeps the historical world

Poiron describes the original idea as removing conflict and narrative, then building curated tours inside the same environment. The player can access the full map of Egypt, travel freely and then enter guided learning experiences

This is a meaningful design shift: the core interaction becomes observation, movement and contextual explanation rather than combat or mission completion

Serious game logic

1

The commercial world provides spatial immersion and visual detail

2

Conflict and main narrative pressure are removed for classroom suitability

3

Curated tours turn exploration into structured learning

4

The player becomes closer to a visitor, observer and learner than a conventional action-game protagonist

Game mechanics redirected toward learning
This slide defines the serious game transformation. It is not that the player learns by accidentally playing the main game. The mode is redesigned for educational use
Production process10 / 15
Behind the scenes

Historical expertise was built into the production pipeline

Poiron describes a production process involving historians, Egyptologists, Hellenists and museum documentation. Experts contributed to the interpretation of daily life, Ptolemaic Egypt, language environment and scientific capsules

This makes Discovery Tour different from a game that merely uses history as visual decoration. It tries to translate scholarship into accessible interaction

Scholarship as production input
Mention that this matters academically because accuracy is not only a final visual judgment. It depends on who is involved and how knowledge is adapted
Educational structure11 / 15
Tours as learning units

The content is organized around attention, accessibility and historical themes

The Discovery Tour expanded from an initial plan of 20 tours to 75 tours organized around Egypt, Romans, pyramids, daily life and Alexandria. The design also limits attention load: tours should stay within roughly 20 minutes, with stations taking about 3 to 5 minutes

Curated knowledgeHistorian-written material is adapted into shorter, accessible texts
Spatial anchoringLearning occurs in the place being described, not outside the world
Museum supportObjects and documentation connect the 3D reconstruction to material evidence
Content design is also attention design
This slide connects Poiron to Bjørner. It shows clearer learning structure than many generic historical games, while still needing evaluation of outcomes
Accuracy and accessibility12 / 15
A serious game is not a perfect simulation

Discovery Tour works through controlled compromise

Poiron is explicit about artistic liberties. In the Temple of Ptah tour, the player can enter spaces ordinary people historically could not access. The game uses this nonrealistic access to explain the restriction

The game also censors nudity on Greek statues for a global classroom audience. This is not historical realism. It is a design decision shaped by age rating, cultural circulation and educational reach

Accuracy is negotiated, not absolute
This is the main critical slide for the Discovery Tour section. The point is not to attack the game but to show that educational design involves trade-offs
Linking both papers13 / 15
Synthesis

Discovery Tour answers several design weaknesses, but not the whole evidence problem

What it answersIt gives a rare high-budget example of expert-informed content, strong immersion, structured tours and classroom-suitable design
What remains openIt does not by itself prove learning effectiveness. Motivation, interest and visual quality still need to be separated from learning outcomes

The Discovery Tour is best understood as a highly developed educational design case, not as automatic proof that historical games improve learning

Design quality and evidence quality are different questions
This is probably the most important academic synthesis. It protects the argument from becoming marketing for Ubisoft
Evaluation proposal14 / 15
How it could be studied

A strong classroom study should separate engagement from learning

Following Bjørner’s critique, a useful evaluation of Discovery Tour would need a clear learning objective, a control group, pre/post knowledge tests and a delayed follow-up. It should also measure whether students can explain historical context rather than only recall tour facts

This would test the educational effect of the serious game more directly than measuring whether students liked the experience

Possible research design

A

Define a narrow unit, for example pyramid workers, mummification or temple access

B

Compare Discovery Tour with a text-based or video-based lesson on the same content

C

Use pre/post and delayed tests with factual and interpretive questions

D

Add observation or interviews to understand how students used the space

Conclusion15 / 15
Final claim

Discovery Tour is a strong serious game because it converts historical spectacle into structured learning

Its value comes from the combination of AAA visual production, expert collaboration, curated tours and museum-supported documentation. It shows what high-quality historical serious gaming can look like when educational design is taken seriously

At the same time, Bjørner’s review prevents an easy conclusion. The game is convincing as an educational design case, but its measurable learning impact still depends on careful classroom evaluation

Conclusion: design strength with evaluation caution
End with the balanced thesis: Discovery Tour is an unusually mature example of serious gaming, but academic analysis should still distinguish design excellence from proven learning outcomes
